# Readiness and independence

## What readiness measures

New and renewed readiness assessments apply current Type I substantive criteria.
Progress records distinguish evidence checked, earlier completion retained with
its recorded basis and date, and explicit customer confirmation labelled as
evidence not checked. Readiness progress does not establish an audit conclusion.
A policy describes a practice; a completed activity needs its required records.
Existing evidence is considered first. Where confirmation is permitted, it must
be the customer's explicit decision; an AI's statement, silence or a general
request to continue is insufficient. Saved gaps still need supporting evidence.
A complete empty source can support a
no-occurrence conclusion only when the check allows it and nothing was due.
Type II separately covers the applicable observation period. Future records
do not need to exist during readiness.

The customer's AI inspects local files with permission. Chiaro receives file
references, fingerprints, inspection notes and assessment results. Policy drafts
require management's adoption of the exact saved version before they can support
readiness credit.

Verdict, progress and its basis appear in the judgment kit, the area checklist
and the checks export; portal pages show each check's status and overall
progress. Display and bookkeeping changes preserve saved work.
Contradictory or expired evidence, changed scope, and an adoption that is absent
or withdrawn can withdraw credit for the affected checks. Earlier evidence and
records remain available, and a completed service stays Complete. Reconnecting does not erase
received work or prove that an unsaved local action reached Chiaro.

Existing examinations retain their recorded library, evidence, original dates
and schedules. Type II keeps two collection rounds and automatic emails.
Readiness and audit evidence are separate records; the examining firm assesses
whether the required audit evidence is sufficient.

## Who makes the decisions

Management chooses its controls, owns their design and operation, determines
what to adopt, and evaluates the results. Chiaro presents criteria, evidence
requirements and options. Where a required document does not exist, the
customer's AI may draft one for management to review, change and adopt. The
customer's AI acts under the customer's direction, and responsibility stays with
management, including for any document drafted this way.

The examining firm separately evaluates the evidence and decides its audit
conclusions. In judging a check, it considers what SOC 2 itself and the
company's own written policy require. Readiness progress and automated checks
are not audit conclusions and do not authorize a report.

Reference: [AICPA Code of Professional Conduct, sections 1.295 and
1.297](https://pub.aicpa.org/codeofconduct/ethicsresources/et-cod.pdf).
